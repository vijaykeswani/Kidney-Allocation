The task: who should receive a kidney?

Why this is a hard decision

Kidney transplantation is the best treatment for many people whose kidneys have failed. But there are far more patients waiting for a kidney than there are kidneys available. So when a kidney becomes available, someone has to decide which patient should receive it — and the patient who does not receive it keeps waiting.

There is no single obviously right answer. Different people care about different things: how long a patient has already waited, how many years of life the transplant would give them, how likely the new kidney is to be rejected, whether other people depend on them, and more. These considerations can point in different directions, and reasonable people weigh them differently. That is what makes this a moral dilemma: any choice favors one patient at the expense of another, and there is no formula that everyone agrees on.

This study is not about finding a “correct” answer. We are interested in your judgment.
